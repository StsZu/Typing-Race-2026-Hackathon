Count nouns can be pluralised, usually by adding s: one cat, two cats, a student, many students. Noncount nouns normally cannot: milk, water, money and food stay as they are, because they name quantities that do not divide into separate units. The distinction changes the words you put in front. Some and any work with both kinds. Much goes with noncount nouns and many with count nouns, so it is many books but much water. Little and few split the same way: little time, few minutes. That is why many moneys sounds wrong to every English speaker.
