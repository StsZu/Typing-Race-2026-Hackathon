English has several ways to talk about the future. We use will for decisions made at the moment of speaking and for predictions, as in I will help you and It will rain tomorrow. We use going to for plans and intentions, for example I am going to study medicine. The present continuous describes fixed arrangements, as in We are meeting at six. The present simple is used for timetables, such as The train leaves at nine. The right choice depends on how certain or planned the future action is.
