The indefinite articles a and an signal that a noun is indefinite: it refers to any member of a group. We use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound, and both work only with countable nouns. The definite article the points to a specific or particular noun, so let us read the book means one book we both know, while let us read a book means any book at all. Noncount nouns take the or no article, but never a or an: we say a glass of milk rather than a milk. Getting this right takes practice, because many languages have no articles.
