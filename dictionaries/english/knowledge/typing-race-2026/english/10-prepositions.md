A preposition opens a prepositional phrase, which runs from the preposition to the noun that is its object. Such phrases work as modifiers: they behave like adverbs or adjectives and tell us more about a verb, an adverb or another adjective. For single points in time we use on, at and in, and for longer stretches since, for, by and during. Place is described with in, inside, on and at; height with over and above or under, underneath, beneath and below; nearness with near, by, next to, between, among and opposite. Many verbs also insist on one particular preposition, and that pairing has to be learned word by word.
