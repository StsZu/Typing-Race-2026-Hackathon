In the active voice the subject of the sentence performs the action expressed in the verb, as in the dog bit the boy. The passive turns that around and always uses a form of be — am, is, was, were, are or been — so the same event becomes the boy was bitten by the dog. Outside science, the passive is worth avoiding: it lengthens sentences and can make prose seem flat and uninteresting. In scientific writing it is readily accepted, because it lets you report findings without putting personal pronouns or researchers' names in the subject.
